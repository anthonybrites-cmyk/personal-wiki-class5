# Goodwill Accounting

Goodwill is the intangible asset recognized when a firm is bought as a going concern: the premium the buyer pays over the net fair value of the target's identifiable assets and liabilities. It arises only through an acquisition and cannot be self-created. Under US GAAP and IFRS, public companies never amortize it; instead it is tested each year and impaired if its value falls, a rules-based treatment the article calls controversial.

## Key facts

- It is recognized only through an acquisition; it cannot be self-created. ([[raw/Wikipedia - Goodwill (accounting)|line 12]])
- To calculate goodwill, the fair market value of the acquired company's identifiable assets and liabilities is deducted from the purchase price. ([[raw/Wikipedia - Goodwill (accounting)#Calculating goodwill|§ Calculating goodwill]])
- In the example, company A paid $20 for company B, whose net assets were $9, so goodwill would be $11 ($20 − $9). ([[raw/Wikipedia - Goodwill (accounting)#Calculating goodwill|§ Calculating goodwill]])
- Under U.S. GAAP and IFRS, goodwill is never amortized for public companies, because it is considered to have an indefinite useful life. ([[raw/Wikipedia - Goodwill (accounting)|line 14]])
- Private companies in the United States may elect to amortize goodwill over a period of ten years or less under an accounting alternative from the Private Company Council of the FASB. ([[raw/Wikipedia - Goodwill (accounting)|line 14]])
- If the fair market value falls below the historical cost, an impairment must be recorded to adjust it down to fair market value, but an increase in fair market value would not be recognized. ([[raw/Wikipedia - Goodwill (accounting)|line 14]])
- Since 2001, U.S. Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (FAS 141) no longer allows the pooling-of-interests method. ([[raw/Wikipedia - Goodwill (accounting)#History and purchase vs. pooling-of-interests|§ History and purchase vs. pooling-of-interests]])

## Related notes

- [[FASB]] — removed goodwill amortization (FAS 142) as a concession after ending pooling-of-interests; its Private Company Council lets private companies amortize
- [[Intangible Asset]] — goodwill is classified as an intangible asset because it cannot be seen or touched
- [[Acquisition Premium]] — goodwill reflects the premium the buyer pays over the net value of the other assets
- [[Impairment Testing]] — goodwill is tested every year and written down, never up
- [[International Financial Reporting Standards]] — has forbidden goodwill amortization since 2005-01-01
- [[US GAAP]] — no amortization for public companies since FAS 142; pooling-of-interests ended by FAS 141
- [[Business Valuation]] — businesses are bought on estimated future cash flows, not the fair value of their assets, which is why goodwill exists
- [[Contingent Value Rights]] — another tool from acquisitions: payments that depend on how the target turns out
