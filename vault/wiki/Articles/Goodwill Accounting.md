# Goodwill Accounting

The document addresses how goodwill is recognized when a firm is purchased, detailing the calculation method and its classification as an intangible asset. The conclusion is that goodwill is subject to impairment testing under certain accounting standards.

## Key facts

- Goodwill is recognized when a firm is purchased as a going concern. ([[raw/Goodwill (accounting)|line 12]])
- To calculate goodwill, the fair market value of acquired assets and liabilities is deducted from the purchase price. ([[raw/Goodwill (accounting)#Calculating goodwill|§ Calculating goodwill]])
- If company A acquired company B and paid $20, goodwill would be $11 ($20 - $9) based on the example. ([[raw/Goodwill (accounting)#Calculating goodwill|§ Calculating goodwill]])
- Goodwill is considered to have an indefinite useful life under U.S. GAAP and IFRS for public companies. ([[raw/Goodwill (accounting)|line 14]])
- Companies may elect to amortize goodwill over a period of ten years or less under an accounting alternative from the Private Company Council of the FASB. ([[raw/Goodwill (accounting)|line 14]])
- Goodwill can only be impaired under current GAAP standards. ([[raw/Goodwill (accounting)#Amortization and adjustments to carrying value|§ Amortization and adjustments to carrying value]])

## Related notes

- [[FASB]] — They removed the option to use pooling-of-interests method for goodwill amortization
- [[Private Company Council of the FASB]] — They established an accounting alternative for amortizing goodwill in private companies
- [[International Financial Reporting Standards]] — They forbid the amortization of goodwill as of 2005-01-01
- [[Intangible Asset]] — Goodwill is classified as an intangible asset on the balance sheet
- [[Acquisition Premium]] — Goodwill represents the premium the buyer pays over the net value of other assets
- [[Impairment Testing]] — Management must determine if an impairment is required when fair market value falls below historical cost
