# FIFO and LIFO Accounting

The document discusses FIFO and LIFO accounting methods used for inventory and financial matters. It explains that FIFO records the oldest inventory first, while LIFO records the most recently purchased items first. The conclusion notes that the difference between the two methods is called the LIFO reserve.

## Key facts

- If Foo Co. sells 210 units in November, FIFO expenses the cost of the first 100 units at $50 and the remaining 110 units at $55. ([[raw/FIFO and LIFO accounting#FIFO|§ FIFO]])
- Under FIFO, the total cost of sales for November would be $11,050. ([[raw/FIFO and LIFO accounting#FIFO|§ FIFO]])
- Under LIFO, the total cost of sales for November would be $11,800. ([[raw/FIFO and LIFO accounting#LIFO|§ LIFO]])
- The difference between the cost of an inventory calculated under the FIFO and LIFO methods is called the LIFO reserve. ([[raw/FIFO and LIFO accounting#LIFO|§ LIFO]])
- The LIFO reserve in the example is $750, calculated as $5250 - $4500. ([[raw/FIFO and LIFO accounting#LIFO|§ LIFO]])
