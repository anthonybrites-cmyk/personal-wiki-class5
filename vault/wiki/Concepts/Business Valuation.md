# Business Valuation

Businesses, when purchased, are valued based on estimates of future cash flows and prices negotiated by the buyer and seller, and not on the fair value of assets and liabilities to be transferred by the seller. ([[raw/Wikipedia - Goodwill (accounting)#Controversy|§ Controversy]]) A privately held software company may have net assets valued at $1 million while the company's overall value (including customers and intellectual capital) is valued at $10 million. ([[raw/Wikipedia - Goodwill (accounting)#Modern meaning|§ Modern meaning]]) CVRs are granted when the acquiring company does not wish to pay for a product that might not work, whereas the acquired company wants to get full value for its assets. ([[raw/Wikipedia - Contingent value rights#Forms|§ Forms]]) To determine the value of these rights, analysts will apply a modified option pricing model based on the probability of the event, the time horizon specified, and the corresponding payout rules. ([[raw/Wikipedia - Contingent value rights#Valuation|§ Valuation]])
