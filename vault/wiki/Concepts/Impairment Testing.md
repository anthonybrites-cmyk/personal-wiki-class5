# Impairment Testing

Impairment testing involves determining the fair value of reporting units and comparing it to the carrying value; if the fair value is less than the carrying value, the goodwill value must be reduced so that the carrying value equals the fair value, with the impairment loss reported on the income statement and the adjusted goodwill reported on the balance sheet. ([[raw/Goodwill (accounting)#Amortization and adjustments to carrying value|§ Amortization and adjustments to carrying value]]) Goodwill is never amortized for public companies under U.S. GAAP and IFRS, and management is responsible for valuing goodwill annually to determine if an impairment is required when the fair market value falls below the historical cost. ([[raw/Goodwill (accounting)|line 14]]) Goodwill is no longer amortized under U.S. GAAP (FAS 142) and is only impaired under these GAAP standards. ([[raw/Goodwill (accounting)#Amortization and adjustments to carrying value|§ Amortization and adjustments to carrying value]])
