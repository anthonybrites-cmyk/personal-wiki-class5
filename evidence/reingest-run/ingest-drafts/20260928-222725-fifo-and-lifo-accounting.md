# FIFO and LIFO Accounting

The document discusses FIFO and LIFO methods for inventory and financial matters. It explains how FIFO records the oldest inventory first, and LIFO records the most recently purchased items first. The conclusion notes that the difference between the two methods is called the LIFO reserve.

## Key facts

- If Foo Co. sells 210 units during November, the total cost of sales under FIFO would be $11,050. ([[raw/Wikipedia - FIFO and LIFO accounting#FIFO|§ FIFO]])
- Under FIFO, the total cost of sales for November would be $11,050. ([[raw/Wikipedia - FIFO and LIFO accounting#FIFO|§ FIFO]])
- The ending inventory under FIFO would be calculated at $5250. ([[raw/Wikipedia - FIFO and LIFO accounting#FIFO|§ FIFO]])
- Under LIFO, the total cost of sales for November would be $11,800. ([[raw/Wikipedia - FIFO and LIFO accounting#LIFO|§ LIFO]])
- The balance sheet would show $4500 in inventory under LIFO. ([[raw/Wikipedia - FIFO and LIFO accounting#LIFO|§ LIFO]])
- The difference between the cost of an inventory calculated under the FIFO and LIFO methods is called the LIFO reserve. ([[raw/Wikipedia - FIFO and LIFO accounting#LIFO|§ LIFO]])

## Related notes

- [[Cost of Goods Sold]] — FIFO and LIFO give different COGS for the same sales: $11,050 vs $11,800 in the Foo Co. example
- [[LIFO Reserve]] — the FIFO–LIFO inventory difference, the amount of taxable income LIFO has deferred
- [[International Financial Reporting Standards]] — IFRS banned LIFO
- [[US GAAP]] — LIFO is used only in the United States, under GAAP
- [[Save LIFO Coalition]] — argues for keeping LIFO; says one third of American companies use it
